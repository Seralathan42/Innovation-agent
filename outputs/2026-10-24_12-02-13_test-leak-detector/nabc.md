# NABC summary: Self-calibrating acoustic leak detection for district heating pipes

*Confidential. Based only on the disclosure of 1 October 2026 (marked as a fictional test case). No web, patent or market search was done.*

> ⚠️ **Urgent:** a conference paper was submitted on 15 Sept 2026 (decision in Dec 2026). Europe has no grace period. Check whether anything is already public, and file a priority application before publication.

**Need:** District heating operators can't find leaks in buried pipes easily. Alarm wires are often broken, drone flights only work in winter, and listening with ground microphones is manual. Finding a leak "can take days of digging." Loss and repair costs are not stated. The trial energy company has asked about a larger pilot in 2027. No other customers have been contacted.

**Approach:** Battery sensors clamped onto pipes in existing valve chambers (300–800 m apart) record at night. They also send test pulses to each other to measure each section's sound speed and damping. This removes the hand-entered pipe values (10–20% wrong) that throw off today's correlators. Possible routes: a spin-off (the founder's wish), a licence to a logger vendor, or a sale.

**Benefits per costs:** Field trial position error was 2.1 m vs 9.4 m for a manual correlator (about 4.5× better). The lab rig detected 0.5 l/min leaks. Parts cost about €120 per sensor (roughly €150–400 per km), with an estimated 5-year battery life. There were 2 false alarms in 4 months, both from a pump station. Price and customer payback are not yet known.

**Competition:** Alarm wires, drones, ground microphones, and acoustic correlators and loggers (likely fast followers). Fibre-optic and thermal imaging also need checking. The edge depends on getting a patent, and no patent search has been done.

**Team and readiness:** Staff scientist (60% share, will stay in research) and doctoral researcher (40% share, wants to found a company in 2027). The team has no business or sales skills. Estimated TRL 5–6, funded by a Research Council of Finland project (2024–27). Possible next steps: Aalto commercialisation funds for the patent, then Business Finland R2B.

**Key risks:** publication before filing; little field evidence (3 known leaks, 6 sensors); false-alarm rate at scale; one founder; unclear IP and data terms with the energy company.
