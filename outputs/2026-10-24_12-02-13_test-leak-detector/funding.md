# Funding report: self-calibrating acoustic leak detection for district heating pipes

*Note: the disclosure is marked as a fictional test case. I treated it as a real invention. All searches used only general terms such as "district heating", "acoustic leak detection" and the names of funding programmes.*

## Urgent: protect the IP before the paper is published

The conference paper was submitted on 15 September 2026, before the disclosure, and the decision is due in December 2026. Once it is published, or posted online, the self-calibration idea is no longer new and cannot be patented. No patent search has been done. Kamstrup already holds patents on acoustic leak detection in district heating networks ([example](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12687263)).

What to do:
- Run a quick novelty and freedom-to-operate search now.
- Check whether the conference posts submitted or accepted papers online.
- Decide by early December 2026 whether to file a Finnish or EP priority application before publication.

All the commercialisation funding below depends on having a protected, licensable invention.

## 1. Where the invention stands and what the next step needs

**Development stage: about TRL 5–6.**
- **Lab rig (DN100, 40 m):** detected simulated leaks of 0.5 l/min, with position error under 1 m.
- **Field trial (2.3 km, 6 sensors, 4 months):**
  - It found 3 known leaks with a mean position error of 2.1 m. The manual correlator on the same data was off by 9.4 m.
  - There were 2 false alarms, both caused by a pump station.
- **Hardware:** sensors built from off-the-shelf parts, about €120 per sensor, with an estimated 5-year battery life.

**Gaps:**
- **IP:** there is no patent search or filing yet, and the publication deadline is close (see above).
- **Product:** the sensors are research prototypes. They are not yet hardened, certified for valve chambers or built for manufacture.
- **Performance:** pump and other noise sources still cause false alarms, and the system has run for one season on one network only.
- **Market:** one friendly operator is interested. There has been no customer discovery, no pricing and no study of competitors (Kamstrup, fixed acoustic logger vendors, Withthegrid, the Swedish consultancy Arne Jensen AB).
- **Team:**
  - The doctoral researcher wants to found a company after the doctorate (2027).
  - The staff scientist will stay in research and could advise.
  - Nobody has business experience, so the team needs a commercialisation lead or business co-founder.

**Next step: a pre-commercialisation project led by the research group.** It would include:
- a larger multi-season pilot with the host energy company in 2027 (about 30–60 sensors, several km, ideally a second operator);
- engineering the sensors towards a product;
- reducing false alarms;
- IP protection;
- market validation in the Nordic, Baltic and Central European district heating markets;
- a spin-off business plan.

**Rough needs:**
- **Money:** about €500–800k over 18–24 months. Most of it is staff (2–3 people including a commercialisation lead). Sensor hardware is cheap, at €5–10k per pilot. Patent costs are €5–10k for a priority filing and €50k or more through PCT and national phases.
- **Partners:** the host energy company, preferably with a letter of intent and some cash or in-kind contribution, one or two more operators, possibly a sensor or electronics manufacturer, and a business mentor.
- **Time:** the spin-off would be realistic in late 2027 to 2028.

**What this shows:**
- The project databases returned **no project funded for acoustic leak detection in district heating specifically**. That suggests open space for the invention, though database coverage is incomplete.
- Funded activity is concentrated in **drinking-water** networks (Loop Sense, WATER-LDL, RAINDROP). The same technology could later be offered to water utilities as a second market.

**Competitors and IP holders:**
- **Kamstrup:** patents on acoustic leak detection through heat meters in district heating networks. Check these for freedom to operate.
- **Fixed acoustic logger vendors** for water networks.
- **Withthegrid:** monitors district heating pipes, but not acoustically.
- **Arne Jensen AB** (Sweden): acoustic leak finding for district heating, reported to be developing permanent monitoring.

**Possible partners:**
- The host energy company, and other Finnish and Nordic operators (reachable through Finnish Energy / Energiateollisuus).
- The RAINDROP and WATER-LDL research groups, for a CETP consortium.
- A Nordic pipe or meter maker as a later licensee or channel partner.
