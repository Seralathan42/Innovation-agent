# First screening: water-based lignin binder for lithium-ion battery cathodes (NMC811)

**Disclosure:** Dr. A. Example and Prof. B. Example, Department of Chemical and Metallurgical Engineering, dated 15 Sep 2026
**Status:** confidential and unpublished. A journal manuscript is planned for spring 2027.
**Nature of this document:** a first desk screening, not a legal opinion. I searched only with general terms (lignin, binder, cathode, aqueous, NMC). No details of the invention were sent to any search tool.

> **Bottom line:** Using kraft lignin as a water-based binder for NMC cathodes is already published (NTNU, 2023) and patented in part (Borregaard, 2018). So the idea alone cannot be patented. Whatever can be patented is in **the specific chemical modification and how well it works with nickel-rich NMC811**. The disclosure does not describe that modification, so the first step is to get that information from the inventors. If it holds up, file a priority application **before the manuscript is submitted in 2027**.

---

## 1. Technology in plain words

**What it does.** A cathode is made by spreading a paste of active material, carbon and a "glue" (binder) onto aluminium foil. Today the glue is PVDF, a fluorinated plastic. PVDF has to be dissolved in NMP, a toxic and costly solvent that must be recovered in energy-hungry drying ovens. The invention swaps PVDF for a chemically modified kraft lignin, a cheap by-product of pulp mills. The modified lignin dissolves in water, so the paste needs no NMP and no fluorine.

**How it works.** The lignin is modified to make it soluble in water and to make it stick strongly to nickel-rich cathode particles (NMC811). Lignin has many polar groups that can attach to oxide surfaces. Its stiff, aromatic structure helps it hold the coating together.

**Reported results (lab only)**

| Measure | Lignin binder | PVDF in the same lab |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity kept after 500 cycles (coin cells) | 91% | 89% |
| Peel strength | Similar | Reference |
| Batch size | 50 g of binder | — |

The team also expects lower binder cost and lower drying energy, but neither has been measured.

**What is new compared with the state of the art**
- Lignin as a water-based cathode binder has already been shown, but for NMC111 (a low-nickel cathode material), at around 100 cycles.
- Water-based binders for NMC811 already exist. Examples are lithium polyacrylic acid (LiPAA), CMC combined with latex, poly(ionic liquid)s and nanocellulose.
- What looks new is a **modified kraft lignin that works on its own with nickel-rich NMC811**, with long cycle life (500 cycles) matching PVDF.
- NMC811 is much more sensitive to water than NMC111. In water, lithium leaches out of the particles, the paste becomes strongly alkaline and it corrodes the aluminium foil. If the modification solves this, that is the real technical step forward.

**Estimated TRL: 3, moving towards 4**
- It is a proof of concept with experimental data, but only in coin cells with 50 g batches.
- There are no pouch cells or cells with commercial-level loading, no data on rheology at coating-line scale, and no full-cell or safety data.
- The cycling advantage (91% vs 89%) is small. It is probably within normal cell-to-cell variation unless statistics show otherwise.
- TRL 4 would need: repeatable lignin batches (lignin varies between mills), electrodes at commercial loading (at least about 3 mAh/cm²), and multi-layer pouch cells.

## 3. Initial novelty check

**Already known**
- Kraft lignin as the binder in water-processed NMC cathodes, including full replacement of PVDF. Shown by Bryntesen et al. (NTNU/UCL), *Materials Advances* 2023 and *J. Mater. Chem. A* 2023 ([MA](https://pubs.rsc.org/zh-tw/content/articlehtml/2023/ma/d2ma00850e), [JMCA](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2023/ta/d2ta08606a)).
- Water-soluble modified lignin (sulfonated) as an electrode binder or dispersant, alone or with CMC (Borregaard family).
- Kraft lignin modified with carboxymethyl or sulfomethyl groups as a water-based battery binder (*ACS Appl. Polym. Mater.* 2025, carbon electrodes) ([link](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsapm.4c04099)).
- Lignin binders for LiFePO4 cathodes and graphite anodes (Lu et al., *Materials* 2016) ([link](https://www.academia.edu/127646921/Lignin_as_a_Binder_Material_for_Eco-Friendly_Li-Ion_Batteries)).
- Water-based processing of NMC811 using other binders, and controlling paste pH with added acid or LiOH/PAA ([AIT](https://publications.ait.ac.at/en/publications/aqueous-manufacturing-of-ni-rich-cathodes-using-polyacrylic-acid/), [MEET Münster](https://www.uni-muenster.de/MEET/en/presse/news/aqueous-processing-cathode-materials.shtml), [nanocellulose, AFM 2025](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adfm.202413865)).
- The general benefits (no NMP, no fluorine, lower drying energy) are well known and cannot be patented.

**Possibly new (cannot be judged without the chemistry)**
- **The specific modification route** and the structure it produces: which functional groups, how many, and what molecular weight range.
- A modified lignin that by itself stabilises NMC811 in water. For example, it might buffer the paste pH, protect particle surfaces or reduce lithium leaching, without added acid or CMC.
- An electrode or cell defined by that binder together with nickel-rich NMC (≥80% nickel), with cycling or peel properties that match or beat PVDF.
- The process window: binder content, pH and drying temperature.

The patent argument will depend on showing the effect is **unexpected** compared with NTNU 2023 (NMC111) and the Borregaard filing. Side-by-side tests against unmodified kraft lignin, lignosulfonate and CMC on NMC811 would make the case much stronger.

## 4. Freedom to operate

- **Borregaard family (WO2019180077):** the main risk.
  - Claim 1 covers any electrode composition with 5–95% water-soluble lignin plus 5–95% CMC, and is not limited to the anode. **If the Aalto formulation contains CMC, it may fall inside this claim.**
  - The EP3544099 filing has ceased. EP3769360, CN111868978 and US20200411873 were pending at the last update I found. Granted claims may be narrower, which I could not check.
  - Where it may be in force: Europe (the EP validation countries), the US and China if granted. Expiry would be around 2039.
  - Action: check status on the EPO Register, USPTO Patent Center and CNIPA.
- **Water-based binder and pH-control patents** (Zeon, JSR, Solvay, and others on water-based NMC processing): these may cover binder mixes with latex or PAA, or paste pH control. They only matter if the product uses such additives.
- **Freedom to operate for a lignin-only binder looks reasonably open** based on this screening. That conclusion is low-confidence until a professional search covers Chinese, Japanese and Korean filings.

## 5. Competing technologies and companies

**Water-based cathode binders**
- Zeon (Nipol/BM latex), JSR, Ashland (AquaSolve), Solvay/Syensqo, and Chinese suppliers of polyacrylonitrile-type binders.
- Academic and early-stage alternatives: LiPAA, poly(ionic liquid)s (CIC energiGUNE/UPV-EHU, [link](https://addi.ehu.es/handle/10810/67338?show=full)), nanocellulose binders.
- PFAS regulation in the EU is pushing the move away from fluorinated binders ([market overview](https://www.futuremarketsinc.com/?p=12821)).

**Dry electrode processing**
- This is the strongest competitor, because it removes the solvent completely.
- Tesla reportedly runs dry-coated cathodes in mass production for the 4680 cell, using a PTFE/PVDF binder ([report](https://english.news18a.com/m/news/english_259603.html)). PTFE is still fluorinated.
- Others: AM Batteries, LiCAP, Fraunhofer, LG Energy Solution.

**Lignin and wood in batteries**
- Stora Enso (Lignode, hard-carbon anodes; partnership with Northvolt).
- Borregaard (lignosulfonates).
- UPM and Södra (kraft lignin supply).
- Ligna Energy, Sweden (lignin as the active material in a different kind of storage device).
- Research groups: NTNU (most directly competing), KTH, and the University of Chicago, which cites Borregaard in WO2025165669.

**Possible partners or licensees**
- The interested Finnish pulp company as lignin supplier.
- Binder formulators such as Syensqo, Arkema or Borregaard.
- Cathode or cell makers in Europe and the Nordics.

## 6. IP recommendation

**Investigate further now, then probably file a patent application before publication.** Use know-how protection alongside it for process details.

1. **Get the modification chemistry from the inventors** (confidentially). Ask what the modification is, how it differs from sulfonation, carboxymethylation and sulfomethylation, and whether CMC or other additives are used.
2. **Commission a professional novelty and freedom-to-operate search** on that chemistry. It should cover CPC H01M4/62 and C08H6, the Borregaard family claims and legal status, and Chinese, Japanese and Korean documents.
3. **If the chemistry is distinct**, file a Finnish or EP priority application well before the manuscript is submitted in spring 2027. It should claim:
   - the modified lignin,
   - the binder for nickel-rich cathodes,
   - the electrode or cell,
   - the slurry process.

   Generate the comparison data needed to support the claims before filing (see section 3).
4. **If the chemistry turns out to be a known modification**, patent protection will be weak. Keep the process and batch-qualification details as know-how and publish the science.
5. **Commercial readiness is low** (TRL 3; no business-experienced team member; no battery-maker contact). Consider a Business Finland Research to Business project to:
   - reach pouch cells,
   - test repeatability across lignin batches,
   - build a business case with the pulp company.

**Where a professional search is needed:** the Borregaard claim scope and legal status, Chinese filings on lignin binders, and novelty once the chemistry is known.
