# NABC Summary: Water-based lignin binder for lithium-ion battery cathodes

*Source: invention disclosure dated 2026-09-15 (the file is marked "fictional test example"). I had no web search tool, so this summary uses only the disclosure. Statements marked [field knowledge] come from general knowledge of the battery industry, not the disclosure. Check them before the meeting.*

**One-sentence summary:** A chemically modified kraft lignin, a pulp-mill by-product, replaces the PVDF binder in nickel-rich lithium-ion cathodes, so the cathode slurry can be water-based instead of using NMP.

---

## Need
- **Who has the problem:** Companies that make lithium-ion cells and cathodes. Today they almost always use a PVDF binder dissolved in NMP, which is toxic and expensive. The NMP has to be recovered in drying ovens that use a lot of energy.
- **How important it is to them:** The disclosure points to solvent cost, drying energy and toxicity. It does not give numbers for how much NMP handling costs per GWh, what share of electrode production cost it is, or how strong the regulatory pressure is: **Not stated.**
- [Field knowledge] Pressure is growing on fluorinated polymers (PFAS) and on NMP as a reprotoxic solvent, especially in the EU. Removing both could matter as much as the cost saving. This should be checked.

## Approach
- **Solution:** Kraft lignin, chemically modified to dissolve in water and bind strongly to NMC811 cathode particles. The slurry is water-based, so no NMP is used and no NMP recovery is needed.
- **How it meets the need:** It removes NMP and PVDF from cathode coating, lowers drying temperature and energy, and uses a cheap feedstock.
- **Status of the IP:** Nothing has been published yet. A manuscript is planned for spring 2027, so **a patent application must be filed before then.** Freedom to operate and what the modification chemistry is: **Not stated.**
- **Likely route to market:**
  1. **Licence**, most likely to a binder or specialty chemicals maker, or to a lignin producer moving into higher-value products.
  2. **Sale or joint development with an existing company.** A Finnish pulp company has shown informal interest as a lignin supplier.
  3. **Spin-off** is possible because the postdoc wants to continue, but no business team exists yet.

  A licence or partnership looks most realistic for now. Battery qualification cycles are long and need a lot of capital.

| Adhesion | Peel strength "similar to PVDF" | Numbers not stated |

The benefit looks strong in principle. **No number for cost per kWh or per tonne of binder has been worked out.**

## Competition
- **Current standard:** PVDF in NMP. [Field knowledge] Major PVDF suppliers include Arkema, Solvay/Syensqo and Kureha.
- **Alternatives:** Comparison with other options is not stated in the disclosure. [Field knowledge]:
  - Water-based binders such as CMC/SBR are already standard for graphite anodes.
  - Several companies and research groups are developing water-based cathode binders, for example acrylic and other polymer types.
  - Dry electrode processing removes solvents altogether and is a likely future competitor.
  - PVDF replacements without fluorine are being developed.
- **Why this could be better:** It is bio-based, cheap and comes from a supply chain that already exists (pulp mills). Its early results on NMC811 match PVDF. Head-to-head comparison against other water-based cathode binders: **Not stated.**

## Readiness and funding
- **Readiness:** Lab scale only (coin cells, 50 g binder batches). Roughly TRL 3–4 (my estimate; the disclosure gives no TRL). Not yet tested in pouch or full-format cells, at larger scale, or on industrial coating lines.
- **Earlier funding:** Business Finland Research to Business pre-study (ended 2026) and an Academy of Finland project.

## Assessment
**Main strengths**
- Targets a clear, well-known cost and environmental problem (NMP and PVDF).
- Cheap feedstock that is available at large volume, and a possible supplier is already interested.
- Early performance matches or beats PVDF on a demanding cathode chemistry (NMC811).
- Nothing published yet, so the IP is still open.

**Main risks**
- [Field knowledge] Nickel-rich cathodes like NMC811 are known to react with water: lithium leaches out, the slurry pH rises and the aluminium foil can corrode. This is often the main barrier to water-based cathode processing, and the disclosure does not address it.
- Coin-cell data over 500 cycles is far from what industry needs to qualify a binder: large cells, more than 1,000 cycles, and calendar ageing.
- Lignin varies from batch to batch, and cell makers need tight, consistent specifications.
- No business lead and no contact with battery makers.
- The planned 2027 publication puts a deadline on filing a patent.
- Crowded field (other water-based binders, dry electrode processing).

**Questions to ask the researchers**
1. How do you handle water sensitivity of NMC811 (lithium leaching, slurry pH, aluminium foil corrosion)? Have you tested larger cells (pouch format), higher loadings and longer cycling?
2. What is new in the chemical modification compared with existing lignin and water-based binder patents and papers? Can we file before the spring 2027 manuscript?
3. Can you estimate the cost at industrial scale: binder cost per kg, and savings on solvent, drying and equipment per kWh compared with PVDF/NMP?
4. How sensitive is the binder to differences between lignin batches and sources? What exactly did the pulp company offer?
5. Postdoc: are you willing to lead a spin-off or licensing project? Who could join as a commercial lead, and which battery or cathode makers could we approach?
