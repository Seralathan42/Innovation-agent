# First screening: water-based lignin binder for Li-ion battery cathodes (NMC811)

*First screening only, not a legal opinion. All searches used general terms. Nothing distinctive from the disclosure was entered into any external tool.*

**Bottom line:** Using lignin as a water-based cathode binder is already known. A RISE patent covering it is granted and in force until about 2036. The invention can only be patented if the inventors' specific chemical modification is new and gives a clearly better result on NMC811. The disclosure doesn't describe that modification, so it can't be judged yet. My recommendation is **investigate further, then probably file a priority application before the 2027 manuscript**, after a professional patent search and freedom-to-operate review.

---

## 1. Technology in plain words

**What it does.** A cathode is made by coating a paste of active material, carbon and a binder (a polymer "glue") onto aluminium foil. Today that glue is almost always PVDF, a fluorinated polymer. PVDF has to be dissolved in NMP, a toxic and costly solvent that must be recovered in energy-hungry drying ovens. The invention swaps PVDF for kraft lignin, a cheap by-product of pulp mills. The lignin is chemically modified so that it dissolves in water and sticks well to nickel-rich NMC811 cathode particles. The paste is then made with water, so no NMP is needed.

**How it works.** Kraft lignin is modified so that it becomes water-soluble and has chemical groups that grip the cathode particles. It is mixed with NMC811, carbon and water, coated onto foil and dried at lower temperature than NMP needs.

**What is new compared with the state of the art:**
- Lignin as a water-based binder for cathodes is **already known**: RISE/KTH (patent filed 2015) and the NTNU work on NMC111 (2022–23).
- Chemically modified, water-soluble lignin binders are **known for anodes** (Borregaard, South China University of Technology, Basque Country/Turin) and for cathodes when blended with polyacrylic acid (Great Power, 2019).
- The possibly new part is a **specific modification that works on NMC811**. Ni-rich materials are the hard case for water processing: lithium leaches out, the pH rises and the aluminium foil corrodes. I found no published lignin work on NMC811. The published lignin cathode work stops at NMC111 and LiFePO4.
- **Gap in the disclosure:** it doesn't say what the modification is, how much binder is used, how much active material is loaded per area, or how leaching and corrosion are handled. Novelty depends almost entirely on these details.

**TRL: about 3, at best low 4.**
- Proof of concept has been shown in coin cells, with 50 g binder batches and comparisons against PVDF in the inventors' own lab. That is TRL 3–4.
- There are no pouch or multilayer cells yet, no pilot coating line, no tests at industrial loadings, and no cell maker has tested it.
- The cycling advantage (91% vs 89% after 500 cycles) is small and may be within normal cell-to-cell spread. It needs replicates and error bars.

## 4. Freedom to operate (first indication only)

- **RISE Innventia, EP3266057B1 / US10439204B2 / CN107408665B: the main risk.** It is in force until about 2036 in the US and in the EP countries where it was validated (which countries is unknown; check the EP register and national registers). It probably also covers China and Sweden. Its independent claims cover a water-based slurry and an electrode with lignin binder **where the lignin was pre-treated to remove fractions below 1000 Da that dissolve in electrolyte**. Whether the Aalto lignin is caught depends on two things:
  - Does the Aalto process remove low-molecular-weight fractions in a way that matches the claims, for example by washing, ultrafiltration or a modification step that removes small fractions?
  - Would a court read a chemically modified lignin as "pre-treated isolated lignin"?

  This needs a professional FTO review. Licensing from RISE is a realistic option.
- **Great Power CN110752370A:** relevant only in China and only if it is granted. It covers modified lignin plus PAA in a cathode. It matters only if the Aalto formulation contains PAA, or if a granted claim 1 covers modified lignin alone.
- **Borregaard US20200411873A1:** aimed at anodes and requires CMC. Low risk for a cathode product unless CMC is used.
- **Patents around NMC811 water processing** (additives, coated particles, carbon-coated aluminium foil, pH control) from cathode material and chemical companies weren't searched in depth here. They are a likely second layer of FTO risk.

## 5. Competing technologies and companies

- **Other water-based cathode binders:** CMC/SBR, PAA and its lithium salt, alginate, water-dispersed PVDF and fluoropolymer latexes from fluoropolymer makers, and acrylic latexes. Japanese and Korean binder suppliers are active here.
- **Dry electrode processing** (solvent-free, PTFE-based; Tesla/Maxwell and several start-ups). It removes both NMP and drying, and competes directly on the same cost and sustainability argument.
- **Other ways to make NMC811 water-processable:** lithium sulfate or acid additives in the slurry ([Li2SO4 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10107986/)), surface coatings (Al2O3, TiO2, ZnO), carbon-coated aluminium foil. A review of this area: [RSC Sustainability 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/su/d4su00098f).
- **Lignin companies:**
  - **Stora Enso** has a lignin battery business ("Lignode", hard-carbon anode material, with a pilot plant) and a kraft lignin offtake deal with Södra from 2027 ([Packaging Insights](https://www.packaginginsights.com/news/stora-enso-inks-kraft-lignin-supply-deal-with-sodra-to-replace-fossil-based-anode-materials-at-scale.html), [Elmia](https://www.elmia.se/en/subcontractor/for-visitors/news/stora-enso-eng)). It is a possible partner, or a competitor if it moves into binders.
  - **Borregaard** (lignosulfonates, binder patents).
  - **UPM** (lignin fractionation, cited in RISE's patent).
  - **RISE** (holds the key patent).
- **Academic groups:**
  - NTNU (Bryntesen, Svensson) with UCL: lignin binders for NMC111
  - KTH/RISE (Cornell, Lindbergh)
  - University of the Basque Country/POLYMAT with Politecnico di Torino (Mecerreyes, Gerbaldi)
  - RISE bio-based binder team ([Dobryden et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma16165553))
  - Universidade Federal de Viçosa/UNICAMP in Brazil
  - South China University of Technology (Qiu, Yang)
  - A January 2026 review in Green Chemistry ([RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/gc/d5gc05761b)) says conductivity, variation between lignin batches and long-term stability are still unsolved.
- **Funded projects:** OpenAIRE returned no funded projects specifically on lignin cathode binders. It found only related ones: the EU BALIHT project (lignin redox flow battery) and the Vinnova BATTERFLAI project (lignin in battery-mineral flotation). This suggests the specific niche is not crowded with large public projects, but the database coverage is limited.

## 6. IP recommendation: investigate further, then probably file a priority application before the manuscript

**Why:**
1. **A broad claim isn't available.** "Water-soluble lignin binder for water-processed NMC cathodes" is anticipated by RISE, NTNU and Great Power. Any patent value lies in (a) the specific chemical modification and its process window, and (b) evidence that it solves the NMC811 problem (leaching, pH, aluminium corrosion, cycling at practical loadings) better than the known approaches.
2. **Next steps, in the next 4–6 weeks:**
   - Get a full technical description from the inventors: the reagents and degree of substitution in the modification, molecular weight, any fractionation or washing, binder content, loading, slurry pH, and the type of aluminium foil.
   - Commission a **professional novelty search**, focused on the specific modification chemistry. Use CPC codes H01M4/622, C08H6/00 and C08L97/00, and search Chinese and Japanese documents carefully.
   - Commission an **FTO opinion on the RISE family**, including which EP countries it was validated in.
   - Ask the inventors to add replicated cells, a pouch or multilayer cell, high-loading electrodes and peel and corrosion data. The 2-point advantage over PVDF is not yet persuasive evidence.
3. **If the modification looks new:** file a Finnish or EP priority application **well before the spring 2027 manuscript**. Draft claims around the modified lignin itself, the way it is made, and its use with Ni-rich cathodes. That gives 12 months to find a licensee or partner before deciding on a PCT filing.
4. **Know-how alone is weak here.** The binder's chemistry can be analysed in a finished electrode, so it is hard to keep secret. Process details, such as lignin quality control and slurry recipes, can be kept as know-how alongside a patent.
5. **If the modification turns out to be known:** publish to build the group's reputation and keep collaborative or licensing value through know-how and a partnership. The Finnish pulp company's lignin supply could be one route.
6. **Commercial notes:**
   - The pulp company is a natural first partner, perhaps through a Business Finland co-innovation project.
   - Testing with a cell maker or cathode material maker is needed early.
   - Talk to RISE early about a licence or cooperation.
   - The postdoc's wish to continue is an asset, but the team needs business support before a spin-out is realistic.
   - Check that the Business Finland R2B pre-study reports and Academy of Finland project descriptions don't already disclose the modification publicly.

**A professional patent search and FTO opinion are needed before any filing or commercial decision.**
