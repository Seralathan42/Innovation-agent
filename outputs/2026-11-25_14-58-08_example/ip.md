# Screening: Water-based lignin binder for Li-ion battery cathodes (NMC811)

*First screening only, not a legal opinion. The disclosure is confidential, so online searches used general terms only. Findings from our own database are marked **[own data]**.*

## 1. Technology in plain words

**What it does.** Cathode coatings today use PVDF binder dissolved in NMP, a toxic and costly solvent. The solvent must be recovered in energy-hungry ovens. The invention replaces PVDF with kraft lignin (a pulp-mill by-product). The lignin is chemically modified so it dissolves in water and sticks to nickel-rich NMC811 particles, so the slurry is water-based.

**Reported results (lab only):**
- 91% capacity retention after 500 cycles, against 89% for PVDF.
- Peel strength similar to PVDF.
- Lower binder cost and lower drying energy.

**What is claimed as new.** The disclosure gives no detail of the modification chemistry, so I cannot judge it. The headline idea (a water-soluble lignin binder replacing PVDF/NMP) is not new; see section 3. The possible novelty lies in:
- the specific modification route and the resulting lignin structure;
- the use on high-nickel NMC811 cathodes, where aqueous processing is hard because of lithium leaching, a high slurry pH and aluminium-foil corrosion;
- the performance data.

**TRL: about 3 to 4.** The proof of concept is in coin cells with 50 g binder batches. I see no pouch cells, no pilot slurry coating, no long-term or high-temperature tests and no safety data. Battery makers would need qualification at much larger scale, which takes years.

## 3. Initial novelty check

**Already known (published or patented):**
- Lignin as a water-based binder for Li-ion electrodes (Innventia/RISE 2015, with EP/US/CN grants).
- Modified or water-soluble lignin (sulfonated, carboxylated, amine) as a binder (Borregaard 2018; Guangzhou Great Power 2019 for cathodes).
- Aqueous cathodes with lignin as the only binder. NTNU published this for NMC111 in 2023: Bryntesen et al., [doi:10.1039/d2ma00850e](https://doi.org/10.1039/d2ma00850e) and [doi:10.1039/d2ta08606a](https://doi.org/10.1039/d2ta08606a).
- Kraft lignin modified with carboxymethyl and sulfomethyl groups as a carbon-anode binder (Izaguirre et al. 2025, [doi:10.1021/acsapm.4c04099](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.4c04099)).
- Hydrothermally modified kraft lignin as an aqueous binder (2026, [doi:10.1016/j.electacta.2026.149973](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.electacta.2026.149973)).
- Reviews of aqueous processing and binders for high-nickel NMC (Rolandi et al. 2024, [doi:10.1039/d4su00098f](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4su00098f)).
- The general benefit story (cost, no NMP, lower drying energy).

**Possibly new (cannot be confirmed from the disclosure):**
- The specific chemical modification route and the resulting binder structure.
- NMC811 (high nickel, more water-sensitive than NMC111) with a lignin-only aqueous binder, and the fact that it works without a separate protection step. I found no direct hit, but my search was shallow.
- The performance combination: 91% retention after 500 cycles and peel strength similar to PVDF. Data alone are rarely patentable, but they support an inventive-step argument.

**Overall.** The general concept is not new. Only a narrow claim to a defined modified lignin, or to a specific process or composition for NMC811, might be patentable. Whether that is possible depends on details I do not have.

**Do not publish or disclose before deciding.** The planned manuscript for spring 2027 would destroy novelty if any filing is wanted. Any partner talks, such as with the pulp company, need an NDA.

## 4. Freedom to operate

No single patent I found clearly blocks the invention. All checks are first-pass, and a professional FTO search is needed. This is especially true because claim text was only available for a few documents.

- **Chalmers/Sevastyanova family (WO2025053785, EP4774768A1).** This is the main risk to watch. SE patent SE546446C2 is granted. The EP is under examination, with no grant yet. It claims an electrode whose binder is lignin (including kraft) crosslinked with a polyol and a polycarboxylic acid, with NMC among the actives. It would only affect the Aalto binder if that binder uses the same crosslinking chemistry. The WO shows a "non-entry into the national phase" event, so check which countries were actually entered. Watch the EP claims as they are examined.
- **Innventia/RISE family (EP3266057B1, US10439204B2).** Granted, but it is a 2015 filing and its legal events show lapses or expiry (SE expired 2024-10, CN terminated 2025-02, several EP states lapsed in 2025). Verify which states and the US patent are still in force. Its claims could not be read (not available from the EPO). A broad "lignin binder in aqueous slurry" claim would be the most relevant risk if any part is still alive, but expiry is likely in most states.
- **Borregaard.** The EP3544099A1 application was refused in 2020 and has no grant. The related US application (US2020411873A1) is still pending, and EP3769360A1 was examined in 2024. The claims cover water-soluble lignin plus CMC for **negative electrodes**, so the risk is low for a cathode binder that does not use CMC.
- **Guangzhou Great Power CN110752370A.** Rejected, so no right.
- **Chinese university applications.** These are China-only applications with no EP or WO family seen. They matter only for production or sales in China.
- **General PVDF-replacement and aqueous-binder patents** (Lintec, Zeon, Propriety, Seoul National University, Arakawa and others). These claim specific polymers or solvents, not lignin, so they are unlikely to block it. Check them if the formulation adds co-binders such as CMC, SBR or acrylics, or additives.

## 5. Competing technologies and companies

- **Other aqueous or non-fluorinated cathode binders.** These include polyacrylic acid, CMC/SBR, polysaccharides (alginate, chitosan, guar, xanthan), and water-soluble vinyl-ether polymers (Lintec). Players: Zeon, Arakawa, Lintec, Meishan Indigo, Tinci, Arkema, Seoul National University, Hansol.
- **Dry-electrode processing** (no solvent and no binder solution; Tesla/Maxwell approach). It removes NMP in a different way and is a strong competitor.
- **NMP-replacement solvent packages** (Propriety Inc., [own data]) and **NMP recovery systems** (Axima Concept, [own data]).
- **Lignin-based competitors.** Chalmers (Sevastyanova), NTNU (Bryntesen, Svensson, Shearing), RISE (Dobryden), Basque Country/Polymat/Politecnico di Torino (Izaguirre, Gerbaldi), Åbo Akademi (lignin binders for sodium-ion cathodes), and Borregaard, Stora Enso (Lignode) and Chinese universities.
- **Funded projects** (research_projects) show little: only BALIHT (EU, lignin redox-flow battery) and BATTERFLAI (Vinnova, lignin flotation). That is not a close match, so funded work on this exact topic is not obvious from the project database.
- **Aalto position.** Aalto has 12 papers in the broader topic (lignin/binder/Li-ion). The most relevant are lignin nanocomposites, lignin energy materials and polysaccharides for energy storage. It is not a leading group for battery binders.
- **Commercial adoption.** I did not find evidence of lignin binders on the market for NMC cathodes. A battery maker would test them only after a lot more data.

## 6. IP recommendation

**Investigate further, then file only if a narrow claim survives. Do not publish yet.**

1. **Hold the manuscript** until a patent attorney has reviewed the invention. A planned publication in spring 2027 would end the chance to file.
2. **Commission a professional prior-art and FTO search.** It should cover the Innventia, Borregaard and Chalmers families, the Chinese lignin applications, and non-patent literature (NTNU, Basque and Chalmers papers). Claim text was missing for several documents.
3. **Identify what is truly specific.** Ask the inventors for details on the modification chemistry (degree of substitution, molecular weight, pH behaviour with NMC811, lithium-leaching and aluminium-corrosion data) and for comparisons against unmodified lignin and the NTNU and Innventia systems. A narrow patent on a defined modified lignin or a process, or a use claim for NMC811, may be possible. Otherwise keep the formulation as know-how.
4. **Strengthen the data and the team.** Generate pouch-cell and higher-loading data, and test at larger scale. The team has no business person, so one should be recruited (for example through the Business Finland and Aalto programmes) before approaching battery makers or the pulp company.
5. **Commercial route.** The likeliest value is a licence or a joint development with a lignin supplier or binder company, not a stand-alone company. Contact battery makers only under an NDA.

## Patent position score: 2 of 5

The concept of a water-based lignin binder, including for NMC cathodes, is already published and patented. Only a narrow claim on the specific modified lignin or on NMC811 performance may remain, subject to a professional search.
