# Water-based lignin binder for lithium-ion battery cathodes
**Summary:** A chemically modified, water-soluble kraft lignin binder for nickel-rich (NMC811) cathodes. It replaces PVDF and toxic NMP solvent with a water-based slurry (disclosure dated 2026-09-15, fictional test example).

## Need
- **Who:** Cell makers and cathode coaters. Today PVDF is dissolved in NMP, a toxic and expensive solvent, and recovered in energy-intensive drying ovens.
- **Importance:** The disclosure gives no quantified pain such as NMP cost per cell or customer demand. Its stated drivers are cost, energy use and toxicity. Regulatory pressure on NMP and PFAS (PVDF is fluorinated) is my background knowledge, not from the disclosure, and needs checking. No battery maker has been contacted, so the need is unvalidated by customers.

## Approach
- Kraft lignin from pulp mills is modified so it dissolves in water. It binds strongly to NMC811 particles, and the slurry needs no NMP.
- **Route to market:** Not stated. The likely route is a licence or co-development with a binder or material supplier, or with a pulp company that has an interest in lignin valorisation. A spin-off is possible only if a business lead joins. Binders are a qualification-heavy B2B materials business with long cell-maker sales cycles.

## Benefits per costs
- **Performance (own lab, coin cells):** 91% capacity retention after 500 cycles versus 89% for PVDF. Peel strength is similar to PVDF.
- **Cost:** "Fraction of PVDF" cost and lower drying energy, with no numbers given. Not stated: €/kg, €/kWh saving, or modification process cost.
- **Caveat:** A 2-point gain from coin cells is within the range where it may not be significant. No full cells, high loading or rate data are given.
- **Technical risk:** Aqueous processing of Ni-rich cathodes is known to cause aluminium foil corrosion and lithium leaching (see [RSC review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/2026/SU/D5SU00849B)). The disclosure does not mention this.

## Competition
- **Incumbent:** PVDF in NMP, with a large supply chain.
- **Aqueous and PVDF-free binders:**
  - [BASF Licity](https://www.emobility-engineering.com/content/uploads/magazines/EME035/60), an aqueous cathode binder (named in a web search result).
  - PAA-based binders such as LA133, described as drop-in replacements; the supplier is not identified.
  - Nanoramic's [PFAS-free, NMP-free electrode technology](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LCD/brochures/BNXXX_Interview-Nanoramic_battery-chemistry-article_BR_v1.pdf).
- **Own patent data (stored_patents, 100 closest families, filed 2020–2025):**
  - Active applicants: Zeon (11), Lintec (5), Meishan Indigo (5), Sumitomo Seika (5), LG Energy Solution (4), Arakawa (4) and Northvolt (3).
  - Lintec has several water-soluble binder families, for example [WO2025205420A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025205420A1).
  - Meishan Indigo has a non-fluorine binder, [WO2025123978A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025123978A1).
  - Propriety Inc has alternative-solvent slurries to replace NMP, [WO2024102352A2](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2024102352A2).
  - **Closest on lignin:** [WO2025053785A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025053785A1) (Askari Ghotbabadi and Sevastyanova, Sweden) claims an electrode with a binder polymerised from lignin, a cross-linker and a polyol. This is a freedom-to-operate and novelty risk.
- **Lignin in batteries:** Stora Enso's [Lignode](https://www.storaenso.com/en/products/lignin/lignode) is a lignin-based hard-carbon anode made in Finland, not a binder. It shows that Finnish lignin supply and battery interest exist. Academic lignin binder work exists, including [a 2016 study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5456705/) and a [2026 review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2026/gc/d5gc05761b). I found no commercial lignin binder product.
- **Our company database:** stored_companies returned no match for lignin binder companies.
- **Why this could be better:** Bio-based, very low raw material cost and a Finnish supply chain. That advantage is unproven against established aqueous binders.

## Assessment
**Strengths**
- Clear environmental and cost story.
- Performance is at least on par with PVDF in the lab.
- Local, abundant raw material with a willing supplier.
- A motivated postdoc inventor.

**Risks**
- Patent novelty and freedom to operate (the Swedish lignin binder application, the large Asian binder portfolios).
- Al corrosion and Li leaching in aqueous NMC811 processing.
- Marginal performance gain at coin-cell scale.
- Conservative cell makers with long qualification cycles.
- No business lead, and no customer contact.
- Publication planned before any patent is filed.

**Questions for the researchers**
1. Has a patent application been filed? How does your claim differ from WO2025053785A1, and when will the paper appear relative to filing?
2. How do you handle Al corrosion and Li leaching at high pH? Do you have pouch-cell, high-loading, rate and calendar-life data, and how many replicate cells support the 91% vs 89%?
3. What exactly is the modification chemistry, how scalable and costly is it, and what are the real cost and energy-saving numbers versus PVDF/NMP and versus other aqueous binders?
4. What does the pulp company want (supply, co-development, equity), and are there any agreements or IP expectations?
5. What role does the postdoc want (founder, CTO?), and who could be recruited as a business lead? Have you talked to any cell makers or binder suppliers?
