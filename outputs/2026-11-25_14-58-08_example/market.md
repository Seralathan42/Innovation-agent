# Market assessment: water-based lignin binder for Li-ion cathodes (NMC811)

Disclosure dated 2026-09-15. Report date 2026-11-25. Web searches used only general terms, never invention details.

## 1. Possible applications

1. **Drop-in aqueous cathode binder, sold as a specialty chemical to cell makers and electrode-coating lines.**
   - Buyers: cell manufacturers (Western, Korean and Japanese cell makers with NMC lines), and binder or specialty-chemical suppliers who would license or distribute it.
   - The pitch is cost, NMP elimination and lower drying energy. It would be sold per tonne, like PVDF.
   - This is the best application, but qualification takes a long time.
2. **"NMP-free / PFAS-free" cathode process package for new European cell plants and pilot lines.**
   - Buyers: greenfield or pilot-scale lines that can design a water-based cathode process from the start. This avoids retrofitting NMP recovery ovens and the cost of NMP solvent recovery.
   - Finland has a battery-materials cluster: Umicore in Kokkola, and a Chinese-Finnish cathode plant under construction in Kotka (our own data, energy memory.md, citing [Daily Northern](https://www.dailynorthern.com/38452/finland-seeks-european-battery-lead-through-high-value-products/)).
   - Finland's battery industry now favours materials over cell gigafactories (same memory file).
3. **Value-added outlet for kraft lignin for pulp producers.**
   - The pulp company is a supplier and co-developer, not a buyer. It is a partner for feedstock and possibly co-funding.
   - Stora Enso is already the largest kraft lignin producer, with 50,000 t/year capacity ([Stora Enso Lignode](https://www.storaenso.com/en/products/lignin/lignode)). It also sells a lignin-based anode material, so lignin is an established battery topic for Finnish pulp firms.
4. **Possible later extension: sodium-ion and LFP cathodes, and anode or separator coatings.**
   - This is speculative. The disclosure only tests NMC811.

## 2. Market size

**Best application: cathode binder.** Figures are consultancy estimates and are only moderately solid. Scopes and definitions differ, and some pages are vendor summaries.

- **Total Li-ion battery binders, all chemistries and electrodes:** USD 3.94 billion in 2026, USD 6.77 billion by 2032, CAGR 10.3% for 2027-2032. PVDF has a 46.5% share ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/lithium-ion-battery-binders-market)). The same publisher has a separate "electrode binder materials" report at USD 3.17 billion in 2026 and a 16.9% CAGR, so even one publisher is inconsistent.
- **PVDF binder, the incumbent to displace:** 2025 estimates range from about USD 1.2 billion to 2.3 billion ([Emergen](https://www.emergenresearch.com/industry-report/advanced-battery-grade-pvdf-binders-market), [Growth Market Reports](https://growthmarketreports.com/report/pvdf-battery-binder-market), [DIResearch via MarketResearch](https://www.marketresearch.com/Deep-Insights-Research-Co-DIR-v4285/Global-PVDF-Lithium-Battery-Binder-41801105)). One outlier, LP Information, reports USD 4.2 billion for cathode PVDF ([MarketResearch](https://marketresearch.com/LP-Information-Inc-v4134/Global-Cathode-Binder-PVDF-Growth-39753995)). This is the figure to treat as the weakest.
- **Cathode share:** a share of 54.1% for cathodes in 2024 is reported by SNS Insider ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/de/news-release/2025/06/16/3099940/0/en/Lithium-ion-Battery-Binders-Market-to-Reach-USD-8-51-Billion-by-2032-SNS-Insider.html)).
- **My rough estimate (not sourced):** the cathode PVDF-type binder pool is about USD 1-2 billion a year globally.
- **Reachable market:** I could not find a reliable figure. As a framing only, it is the share of cathodes that are nickel-rich (NMC), made outside China, in plants willing to adopt aqueous processing. This is likely a small fraction of the pool in the first 5 years. A bottom-up estimate needs a binder dosage per kWh, a price per tonne and European gigafactory volumes. I have not found those.
- **European volume context:** EU27 extra-EU imports of Li-ion cells (HS 850760) were EUR 28.6 billion in 2025, +25% on 2024, and 90% came from China ([Eurostat Comext DS-045409](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/DS-045409/default/table), via our own energy memory.md). Europe mostly imports cells, so local demand for a European binder is limited until local cell production grows.
- **Eurostat:** my DS-045409 query for the EU import of fluoropolymers (HS 390469, 2024) returned no values, and PRODCOM for cells was also empty according to our memory.md. I could not get a Eurostat market size for binders.
- **Finland:** I found no Finnish binder or cathode-binder market statistics. The Trade Register lists 43 companies under TOL 20160 (plastics in primary forms). Most are unrelated, and I saw no Finnish battery binder maker ([PRH/YTJ query](https://www.ytj.fi)).

## 3. Competitors and alternatives

**Incumbent**
- PVDF in NMP, the industry standard. Suppliers include Solvay and Arkema (both named in research papers as PVDF baselines, not verified as the main market leaders). Chinese PVDF producers are many, for example Juhua ([EP4206241A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DEP4206241A1)).
- PVDF has a long qualification history, which is why it holds the market ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/lithium-ion-battery-binders-market)).

**Existing water-based alternatives**
- BASF Licity, an aqueous binder for electrodes, and PAA-based "LA133", a PVDF replacement ([eMobility Engineering](https://www.emobility-engineering.com/?p=17945); [search result summary](https://www.emobility-engineering.com/content/uploads/magazines/EME035/60)). I did not verify their cathode use or status.
- Aqueous PVDF latices and other acrylic or CMC/SBR systems. CMC/SBR is the standard on graphite anodes, so water-based coating is proven on anodes.
- Cellulose-based binders are in research, for example CNC-COOH ([RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2024/ta/d4ta03305a)).
- Known issues in water-based Ni-rich cathodes: aluminium foil corrosion, lithium leaching from NMC and a high pH ([RSC 2024](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/2026/SU/D5SU00849B), [ORNL](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/performance-different-water-based-binder-formulations-ni-rich-cathodes-evaluated-lini)). The disclosure does not say how these are handled.

**NMP-alternative solvent route**
- Propriety Inc. (US) has alternative solvent and binder packages in place of NMP ([WO2024102352A2](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2024102352A2), our patent data).

**Patent landscape (our own database, stored_patents)**
- Among the 100 closest patent families, the top applicants are ZEON (11), LINTEC (5), Meishan Indigo Tech (5), Sumitomo Seika (5), LG Energy Solution (4), Arakawa Chemical (4) and Northvolt (3). Filings peaked in 2022-2024 and the main classes are H01M, C08L and C08F.
- **Most important finding: closest lignin prior art.** [WO2025053785A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025053785A1) (Askari Ghotbabadi and Sevastyanova, Sweden, filed 2023-09, published 2025-03) claims an electrode whose binder is made from lignin or modified lignin, a cross-linker and a polyol. A freedom-to-operate and novelty analysis is needed before anything else. I only compared at abstract level.
- Other close patents are water-soluble polymer binders from Lintec ([WO2025205420A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025205420A1) and related) and Zeon ([WO2025182989A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025182989A1)).
- A Chinese maker, Meishan Indigo, files non-fluorine binders but with NMP solubility, so it is a PFAS-free binder route that keeps NMP ([WO2025123978A1](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/patent/search?q=pn%3DWO2025123978A1)).

**Finnish / Nordic players (our data)**
- Stora Enso (lignin, Lignode anode, Sunila pilot) is a possible partner or a competitor in lignin-for-batteries ([Stora Enso](https://www.storaenso.com/en/products/lignin/lignode)). Our database lists it with "lignin binders" in its profile, so check whether it has its own binder work.
- Umicore Kokkola and the Kotka cathode plant are potential customers for qualification, as they are cathode-material makers rather than cell makers. They do not coat electrodes, so a binder buyer is further downstream.

**How the invention differs (from the disclosure only):** it is a renewable, low-cost lignin binder, water-soluble through chemical modification, for NMC811, with cycle retention of 91% vs 89% for PVDF after 500 cycles and similar peel strength. The data are lab-scale coin cells only. They give no evidence yet against the aluminium corrosion and lithium leaching issues, which decide whether a water-based cathode works in practice.

## 4. Trends and drivers

- **Regulation (NMP and PFAS):** ECHA has an NMP restriction dossier with total ban and derogation options. I could not confirm its date or status ([ECHA CHESAR document](https://chesar.echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/5161f2bf-503c-2107-ab54-c087017d11c9)). The universal PFAS restriction proposal had a final consultation from 26 March to 25 May 2026 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/europes-pfas-restriction-proposal-moving-forward), [Eurofins bulletin May 2026](https://www.eurofins.com/consumer-product-testing/media-centre/news/chemicals-monthly-bulletin-may-2026/)). Battery makers such as VARTA and Northvolt have lobbied that PVDF and PTFE binders are affected. A PFAS ban or battery derogation outcome is the biggest swing factor. A derogation would remove much of the pull.
- **EU Batteries Regulation:** due diligence is postponed to 2027-08-18 and an Omnibus IV simplification is proposed (our memory.md, citing [EUROBAT](https://eurobat.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Final-EUROBAT-Battery-Omnibus-Paper.pdf), search summary only). Carbon footprint and recycled-content requirements could favour bio-based inputs, but I have not checked this.
- **Cost and energy:** NMP recovery and drying are a real cost, and falling cell prices push cell makers toward cost cutting. Import cell value per kg fell about 21% in 2025 (our calculation in memory.md from Eurostat).
- **Industry caution:** PVDF's incumbency, qualification times of often 1-3 years (my general knowledge, not sourced) and a risk-averse cell industry slow adoption.
- **Supply-chain push:** Europe's reliance on China for cells (90% of import value) and Finland's strategy shift to battery materials ([Daily Northern](https://www.dailynorthern.com/38452/finland-seeks-european-battery-lead-through-high-value-products/)) support a local materials story. The weak side: European cell production is small and has had failures. Our memory.md lists Britishvolt Finland and Johnson Matthey Battery Materials Finland as ceased.
- **Funding fit (our memory.md):** Horizon Europe battery materials call HORIZON-CL5-2027-04-Two-Stage-D2-07 (EUR 48 million, TRL 4-5, first stage 2027-04-14); Business Finland Co-Research (open to 2026-12-18); Horizon bridging call (2027-02-16). Research to Business 2/2026 closed 2026-09-16.

## 5. Open questions for the researchers

1. **IP and novelty:** Have you compared your binder with WO2025053785A1 (lignin binder with cross-linker and polyol, Sweden) and the other lignin-binder literature? Is there a patent application filed? The manuscript is planned for spring 2027, so the first filing must come before any publication. What is the claim?
2. **Technical gaps:** How do you handle aluminium foil corrosion and lithium leaching or high pH in water-based NMC811 slurries? What is the electrode loading, areal capacity, and the electrolyte? Are the results reproducible beyond coin cells, in pouch cells at higher loading and at fast charge?
3. **Fairness of comparison:** Is the 91% vs 89% gap (500 cycles) outside the error margin? How many cells? Is the PVDF reference a well-optimised industrial recipe?
4. **Modification chemistry:** What is the process, cost and yield of the lignin modification, and which reagents? How variable is kraft lignin between pulp mills and batches? Can 50 g be scaled to tonnes?
5. **Cost claim:** What is the cost per kg of the modified binder compared with PVDF? "A fraction" needs numbers, including energy saving in drying.
6. **Customers:** Which cell makers or cathode-material firms might test samples? Is the pulp company willing to sign an NDA, supply lignin and co-fund?
7. **Team:** Is the postdoc willing to lead a spin-out? A business lead or an industrial advisor with battery-materials experience is needed. Would licensing to a chemical company be preferable?
8. **Funding and rights:** What do the Business Finland R2B and Academy of Finland terms imply for ownership? What next-step funding (R2B 2027, Horizon bridging, Co-Research) is realistic?
9. **Other uses:** Does it work for LFP, sodium-ion, or anodes? That could widen the market.
