## 2. Finnish funding options

- **Business Finland Research to Business (R2B)**
  - **Applicant:** a research organisation only, not an individual researcher or group.
  - **Size:** a grant of 80 % of eligible costs, with typical total project costs of EUR 400k–700k.
  - **Timing:** call 2/2026 ran 8 June to 16 September 2026, so it has closed. Decisions are expected in January 2027. Two calls run each year, so watch for the spring 2027 call.
  - **Budget:** EUR 25 M was allocated across the 2026 calls.
  - **Links:** [call 2/2026](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/funding/calls/2026/r2b-funding-call-2-2026/) and [call 1/2026](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/funding/calls/2026/research-to-business--funding-call-12026/).
- **Business Finland Co-Innovation and Veturi ecosystem funding**
  - **What it is:** it funds company–research collaboration projects.
  - **How to access it:** an ecosystem route exists through leading (Veturi) companies. Business Finland set aside about EUR 50 M for ecosystem members.
  - **Timing:** the 2026 calls are in progress. Eligibility and the current windows need checking.
  - **Link:** [funding services overview](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/whats-new/news/2026/what-do-business-finlands-funding-services-look-like-at-the-beginning-of-2026/).
- **Business Finland Sprint**
  - **What it is:** a pilot for small companies with new innovations, replacing the closed Tempo call.
  - **Timing:** the pilot ran 2.3–31.8.2026. Check whether it is continuing.
  - **Use:** relevant once a spin-out company exists.
- **Research Council of Finland**
  - **What it is:** academic research funding and the Proof of Concept-type routes. I did not verify the current calls.
  - **Link:** [aka.fi](https://www.aka.fi)
- **Other Finnish and Nordic sources, not verified in this search**
  - **University innovation services and TTOs:** they run early-stage commercialisation funds.
  - **Foundations:** for example the Finnish energy-related foundations.
  - **Investors:** Finnish deep-tech investors such as Tesi and Voima Ventures, and energy-sector corporate venture arms.
  - **Pilot customers:** Finnish district heating utilities (for example Helen, Fortum, Vantaa Energia) as pilot customers.
- **EIT InnoEnergy and EIT Urban Mobility/Climate-KIC:** these may offer accelerator support. I did not verify them.

## 4. The usual funding path from lab result to company

1. **Lab result and proof of concept.** Funding comes from Research Council of Finland or university money, and often from a doctoral network or an industry-linked project. File an invention disclosure with the university TTO and a first patent filing before publishing. The relevant patent classes are G01M3/24, G01M3/28, F17D5/02 and F24D10/00.
2. **Commercialisation preparation.** The main route in Finland is Business Finland Research to Business. It pays 80 % of costs, typically EUR 400k–700k. It covers customer discovery, field testing and team building, with the aim of starting a company or licensing.
3. **Field validation with a utility.** Find a district heating operator for a pilot. Co-Innovation or ecosystem funding can pay for this. Horizon Europe Innovation Actions (such as the Apply AI energy pilots) need a consortium that includes utilities.
4. **Company formation and early funding.** Business Finland Sprint, or Tempo-type funding if it returns. Add angel and seed investors, and the EIC Accelerator for larger grant-plus-equity rounds. Check the EIC status.
5. **Scale-up.** Commercial sales to utilities and maintenance contractors, plus venture capital. The customer types are district heating operators, energy utilities, municipal owners and maintenance contractors. Competition comes from established alarm-wire and acoustic leak-detection suppliers, so sales to utilities with pilot references are the key milestone.
