"# Energy and storage: knowledge file (first run 2026-11-05, updated 2026-11-09, 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-16)\n\n## Overview\nScope: batteries and storage, renewables, hydrogen, smart grids and district heating, with a focus on Europe and Finland. Grid-scale battery storage is still the fastest-moving area. Pooled project financing (Return, EUR 835 million) and very large 4-hour projects (Elements Green, 1.6 GWh with a TenneT connection) are arriving in Germany. Finland has a high renewable share and a fast-growing storage pipeline, and its district heating is close to fossil-free. Europe depends on imported lithium-ion cells: extra-EU imports rose 25% to EUR 28.6 billion in 2025, and 90% came from China ([Eurostat Comext DS-045409](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/DS-045409/default/table)). Finland's battery industry now advises against mass cell production and points to materials, systems and software instead. Hydrogen support exists (EU Hydrogen Bank), but delivery is uncertain and the next auction is smaller (up to EUR 500 million, planned for December 2026).\n\n## Market size and growth\n- Europe installed 36 GWh of battery storage in 2025 (+48% YoY). The fleet is above 100 GWh. Utility-scale was over 50% of installs, and residential fell to 34% from 49% ([SolarPower Europe via ESS News, 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/06/23/solarpower-europe-reports-record-36-gwh-of-european-storage-installs/)).\n- Forecast: more than 50 GWh in 2026 and 138 GWh in 2030, a 28% CAGR over 2026-30 (SolarPower Europe, medium scenario). EUPD Research is higher at 57 GWh for 2026 (+78%), so the forecasts differ ([ESS News, 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/09/07/europes-storage-market-to-hit-57-gwh-in-2026-says-eupd-research/)).\n- New: EU27 imports of lithium-ion accumulators from outside the EU (HS 850760) were EUR 28.59 billion in 2025, against EUR 22.89 billion in 2024 (+25%). Volume was about 1.62 million t against 1.02 million t (+59%), so the import value per kg fell from about EUR 22.4 to EUR 17.6 (-21%; my calculation). Imports from China were EUR 25.73 billion in 2025, against EUR 19.62 billion in 2024, so China's share rose from 86% to 90% ([Eurostat Comext DS-045409](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/DS-045409/default/table), queried 2026-11-16). PRODCOM sold-production value for EU accumulators (27202300) returned no values for 2022-2025, probably because of confidentiality.\n- Finland storage: Fingrid reported about 1,050 MW in February 2026 (980 MW from 261 systems of 1 MW or more, plus about 70 MW smaller) ([Fingrid, 2026](https://www.fingrid.fi/en/news/news/2026/the-combined-capacity-of-electricity-storages-in-finland-now-exceeds-1000-mw/)). Renewables Finland reports just over 1.6 GW in July, nearly 1 GW under construction and 11.9 GW planned ([Renewables Finland, 2026](https://suomenuusiutuvat.fi/en/nearly-12-gw-of-battery-storage-planned-in-finland-1-gw-under-construction/)). Definitions differ, so treat the numbers with care.\n- Renewable share of gross final energy consumption in 2025: Finland 53.0%, EU27 26.2% (2024: 52.1% and 25.2%). Renewable share of electricity in 2025: Finland 56.6%, EU27 49.9% (2024: 54.3% and 47.5%) ([Eurostat nrg_ind_ren](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/nrg_ind_ren/default/table)).\n- Finland's lithium-ion battery trade (HS 850760) in 2025: imports EUR 847 million (2024: EUR 1,171 million), exports EUR 364 million (2024: EUR 669 million) ([Eurostat Comext DS-045409](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/DS-045409/default/table)). By my calculation that is about -28% for imports and -46% for exports. Import weight fell less (34,724 t vs 38,202 t), which suggests falling prices explain part of the import drop. This fits the EU-wide fall of about 21% in import value per kg. The cause of the export drop was not researched.\n- District heating in Finland used just over 31 TWh in 2025. Emissions fell 38%, fossil fuels and peat were 13% of supply (47% in 2019), electric boilers supplied 8%, and the last coal boiler closed in spring 2025. Electricity use rose 2 TWh to 85 TWh and wind's share rose to 26% ([Finnish Energy, 2026-01-21](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/71751647/energiatilastot-2025-kaukolammon-paastot-putosivat-kolmanneksella-sahko-lahes-fossiilitonta-ja-euroopan-edullisinta)). For 2024, Finnish Energy reports about 32.7 TWh consumed, a 46% market share and about 16,700 km of networks ([Finnish Energy, District heating 2024](https://energia.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/District_heating_2024_updated_20251202-1.pdf); figures from the search summary, PDF not opened).\n- Hydrogen: the third Hydrogen Bank auction (IF25, results 2026-05-07) awarded about EUR 1.09 billion to 9 projects, about 1.1 GW electrolysis ([Hydrogen Europe, 2026](https://hydrogeneurope.eu/high-levels-of-competition-in-3rd-eu-hydrogen-bank-auction/)). Sources differ on the lowest bid (EUR 0.44/kg vs a EUR 0.57/kg range floor); unresolved. Finnish projects are shifting to 2-hour systems; Germany is financing and connecting 4-hour systems (Return; Elements Green 1.6 GWh) ([ESS News, 2026-11-05](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/11/05/return-secures-e835-million-in-portfolio-financing-for-large-scale-battery-storage-in-germany/)).\n- Financing is maturing: pooled portfolio debt (Return, Deutsche Bank and Rabobank), dedicated EU battery funds (Gore Street), tolling agreements (Centrica for AKKU One) and long optimisation contracts (Statkraft and OX2, seven years). In the UK, contracting is reported to be standardising from tolling towards floor and offtake agreements (Energy-Storage.News, 2026-11-13, search summary only). This creates demand for trading, forecasting and optimisation software.\n- Developers sell ready-to-build projects to infrastructure funds and utilities (Fu-Gen to Nala; the 100 MW Teuva sale closed on 2026-09-29, [Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/fu-gen-nala-renewables-close-100mw-bess-finland/)), and lenders are financing Finnish projects (Delta Capacity closed financing for 250 MW / 600 MWh, [Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/nordics-roundup-delta-capacity-closes-finland-financings-ingrid-alpiq-offtake-framework-monssons-first-investment/)).
