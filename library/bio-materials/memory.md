# Bio-based and advanced materials: knowledge file (updated 2026-11-10)

## Overview
Scope: forest-based materials, cellulose and lignin products, bio-based chemicals, advanced and circular materials. Europe and Finland focus. The Finnish forest industry (Stora Enso, UPM, Metsä, Kemira) anchors the sector. Aalto and VTT run the FinnCERES flagship and the Aalto Bioinnovation Centre ([Aalto, FinnCERES](https://www.aalto.fi/en/collaboration/finnceres)). Current state: markets for pulp and fibre are weak and costs are inflated, so incumbents are cutting risky textile-fibre projects and investing in packaging. Policy and EU funding remain supportive. Aalto-linked start-ups keep raising small rounds (lignin, cellulose, foams).

## Trends
- Incumbents are refocusing on packaging and energy. Stora Enso is demerging its Swedish forest assets (H1 2027) and opened a Cellufoam fibre-foam packaging pilot plant in September 2026 (one secondary search summary; verify).
- Metsä cancelled the Kuura textile fibre mill (Kemi) on 2026-08-11 because of Asian overcapacity and low willingness to pay a premium, and is investing about EUR 25 million in a commercial Muoto fibre-packaging line at Äänekoski (start 2028). [Metsä Muoto/Kuura](https://chemxplore.com/news/metsa-group-muoto-fibre-packaging-investment) Metsä's lignin and carbon capture projects continue as planned ([Packaging Insights](https://www.packaginginsights.com/news/metsa-pivots-textiles-fiber-food-packaging.html)).
- Textile-fibre start-ups face capital pressure: Spinnova plans a US IPO of at least USD 15 million against a market cap of about EUR 27 million. [Inderes](https://www.inderes.dk/en/analyst-comments/spinnova-announces-intention-to-pursue-us-ipo-and-dual-listing) Shareholders approved the dual listing plan (reported 2026-09-29) and a EUR 1.5 million bridge financing was reported in early October; no pricing or closing found.
- Lignin valorisation is attracting industrial investors (Teknor Apex's TekVentures, Kiilto) for adhesives and coatings.
- Fully cellulose-based barrier films and coatings (VTT/LUT F3 project) are nearing commercial use in recyclable packaging.
- Raw-material and energy cost inflation weighs on chemicals (Kemira) and legacy paper and fibre (UPM). Paperboard balance is improving and softwood pulp demand is expected to stay subdued (Metsä).
- Bio-based foams, insulation and packaging are the typical Aalto commercialisation targets.

## Regulation and policy
- EU Bioeconomy Strategy adopted November 2025. The Council approved conclusions on 2026-03-17 (one secondary source).
- The Commission is exploring bio-based content requirements and bio-based plastics criteria under PPWR (slated for 2027). Sources differ on how binding these will be; verify with Commission documents.
- Biotech Act II: call for evidence closed 2026-06-10; proposal pending.
- Cefic (June 2026) wants one biomass-content target per product group that counts bio-attributed inputs. Bio-based advocates may disagree.

## Opportunities for Aalto inventions
- Open EU calls: Horizon Europe bridging calls (2027-01-28, EUR 32.5 million for about 13 grants of up to EUR 2.5 million, needs results from a completed Pillar II project, max 6 partners, [call details](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/opportunities/topic-details/horizon-bridging-2027-02-05); and HORIZON-BRIDGING-2027-06-02, deadline 2027-02-16), MAT-PROD-06 circular advanced materials (2027-02-02, back in the search this run), CBE JU CSA (2026-12-31), Forests and Forestry partnership (2027-06-02), and CIRCBIO calls opening 2027-04-20 (deadline 2027-09-22; includes CIRCBIO-09 upcycling/recycling, -03 recycling of complex plastics with biotech, -07 biomass flows).
- Business Finland funding (it funds LignoSphere's coatings piloting until June 2027) and the Aalto R2B route.
- Cellulose films and coatings, bio-foams, insulation, lignin materials (adhesives, coatings), textile fibres, cellulose-based opacifiers (CELLIGHT) and bio-based superabsorbents.
- Partner with incumbents. They need new growth products but are cutting textile-fibre capex, so packaging and fibre-based formed products are the easier pitch than textile fibres. Corporate venture arms (Kiilto, TekVentures, Oji, CMPC) are active early investors.
