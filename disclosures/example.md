# Invention disclosure (FICTIONAL TEST EXAMPLE)

**Title:** Water-based lignin binder for lithium-ion battery cathodes

**Inventors:** Dr. A. Example (postdoc) and Prof. B. Example, Department of Chemical and Metallurgical Engineering

**Date:** 2026-09-15

## Description
Lithium-ion battery cathodes are made by coating a slurry of active material, carbon and a binder onto aluminium foil. Today the binder is almost always PVDF, a fluorinated polymer that must be dissolved in NMP, a toxic and expensive solvent that has to be recovered in energy-intensive drying ovens.

We have developed a binder made from kraft lignin, a by-product of pulp mills, that is chemically modified so that it dissolves in water and binds strongly to nickel-rich cathode particles (NMC811). The slurry is water-based, so no NMP is needed.

## Results so far
- Coin cells with the lignin binder keep 91% of their capacity after 500 cycles, compared with 89% for PVDF in our lab.
- Peel strength of the coating is similar to PVDF.
- Lignin costs a fraction of PVDF, and drying energy falls because water evaporates at a lower temperature than NMP.
- Tested only at lab scale (coin cells, 50 g batches of binder).
